# Module 2 · Server Rules & Expectations

Understanding the server's rules and enforcement philosophy is essential for every staff member. You need to know not only what is and is not allowed, but also how to apply those rules fairly and consistently.

---

## Player Rules

The server rules define the behaviors expected from every player.

They can easily be found in the staff documentation [section 1.3](../../documentation/introduction.md#13-server-rules){ data-preview }.

!!! important "Know the rules"
    Staff members are expected to be familiar with the current player rules before taking moderation action.

---

## Staff Expectations

Staff members have additional expectations beyond those placed on regular players.

These expectations cover how staff interact with players, use their permissions, communicate with other staff, and document their actions.

For the authoritative staff expectations, see staff documentation [section 1.2](../../documentation/introduction.md#12-expectations){ data-preview } and [section 2.2](../../documentation/staff-roles-responsibilities.md#22-staff-conduct-expectations){ data-preview } for conduct expectations.

---

## Enforcement Philosophy

ERRSA MC uses a generally **relaxed enforcement philosophy**.

!!! tip "The basic rule of thumb"
    If something is not harming another player or harming the server, it is generally likely to be allowed.

Examples of behavior that can harm players or the server include:

- Griefing or stealing.
- Harmful or targeted language.
- Economy exploits.
- Lag machines.
- World griefing.
- Cheating or exploiting.

The purpose of rules and punishments is **not** to restrict players unnecessarily. They exist to keep the server fun, fair, and friendly for everyone.

In most situations, a conversation or warning is enough to resolve an issue.

!!! warning "Administrative action should be a last resort"
    Mutes, kicks, bans, and other administrative actions should generally be reserved for serious situations or repeated offenses.

---

### Intent Matters

Staff should distinguish between intentional and unintentional behavior.

For example, a player might build a complex Redstone farm that unexpectedly causes server lag. That is different from a player intentionally building a lag machine for the purpose of making the server difficult for others to play.

The result may look similar, but the circumstances and intent provide important context when deciding how to respond.

---

### When Evidence Is Incomplete

Some situations will not have a completely clear answer.

In more complex cases, ERRSA MC staff should generally consider:

> **"What is most likely to have happened based on the available evidence?"**

This does not mean assuming guilt without evidence. It means using the available information and sound judgment when a situation cannot be proven with absolute certainty.

---

## When Something Becomes a Staff Issue

Staff intervention is generally appropriate when:

- A direct server rule is broken.
- A player is actively harming another player.
- A player is actively harming the server.
- Suspicious behavior requires investigation.
- An inappropriate build or sign is discovered.
- Another situation clearly requires staff attention.

!!! info "Reports are not the only source of staff action"
    Staff do not need to wait for a player report something before addressing a problem. If you discover a clear rule violation or harmful behavior yourself, you should address it appropriately.

---

## Common Rule Violations

The most common violations tend to be behaviors that are easy and quick to perform while providing significant benefit to the offender.

### Stealing & Griefing

Stealing or griefing often takes only a few seconds but can erase hours of another player's progress.

Players may lose:

- Enchanted gear.
- Diamonds and other resources.
- Large builds.
- Farms.
- Other valuable items.

Even when the stolen items may seem insignificant, the impact on the affected player can be substantial and extremely discouraging.

!!! important "Protect the community"
    Because ERRSA MC is intended to be a welcoming community, theft and griefing should be taken seriously even when the material loss may appear small.

### Cheating & Exploiting

Cheating and exploiting can similarly provide a large advantage with very little effort.

Examples include:

- X-ray resource packs.
- Hacked clients.
- Duplication exploits.
- Other unintended mechanics used for personal advantage.

These behaviors can affect not only the individual player but also the server's economy and the fairness of the experience for everyone else.

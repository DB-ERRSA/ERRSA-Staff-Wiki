# Module 3 · Player Support

The most important responsibility of any staff member is being a helpful resource for the community.

Staff should be approachable, knowledgeable, and willing to help players find the information or support they need.

---

## Answering Common Questions

Players will frequently ask questions about:

- Commands.
- Server systems.
- Rules.
- Registration.
- Claims.
- Economy and shops.
- Progression.
- Other player-facing features.

To answer questions efficiently, you need to be familiar with the systems available to players and know where to find additional information when you do not know the answer yourself.

!!! tip "You don't need to memorize everything"
    Being a good staff member does not mean knowing every command or system by memory. Knowing **where to find the answer** is just as important.

---

## Helping New Players

New players are likely to have more questions than experienced players.

The registration process used to verify **ERAU student status** is one of the most common sources of questions.

!!! info "Player registration"
    Review the [player onboarding tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nrxG9c11pdo "Click here to watch the tutorial video!") to familiarize yourself with the registration process, or view the [Player Initialization Plugin](../../plugins/server-and-infrastructure/playerinitialization.md){ data-preview } page for technical details.

New players may also be unfamiliar with Minecraft servers that use plugins and may not know commands such as `/warp`, `/wild`, and `/home`.

Being familiar with the major server systems makes it much easier to help someone get started. We will go over the major systems in more detail in the [next module](mod4.md){ data-preview }.

However, new players are not the only people who need help. Even experienced players may be unfamiliar with a particular feature or need clarification about a rule.

**Staff are here to help the entire community.**

---

## Using the Wiki

ERRSA MC is large enough that no staff member can reasonably be expected to remember everything.

This wiki is designed to be your primary reference when you need information or want to learn something yourself.

It contains information about:

- Server systems.
- Plugins.
- External tools.
- Staff procedures.
- General server principles.
- Troubleshooting common issues.

The wiki is also a **living document**.

!!! tip "Help improve the wiki"
    If you find information that is missing, confusing, outdated, or contradictory, let a Developer or the Server Lead know.

---

## Finding the Right Tool or Plugin

When you encounter a problem, start by identifying **what kind of problem it is**.

| If you need... | Start with... |
|---|---|
| Staff rules or procedures | **Documentation** |
| Training or general understanding | **Learning** |
| Information about a specific plugin | **Plugins** |
| Information about an external service | **Tools** |
| Help diagnosing a problem | **Troubleshooting** |

!!! tip "Not sure where to begin?"
    Start with the **Troubleshooting** section. It is designed to point you toward the relevant plugin, tool, system, or procedure when you know what is wrong but are not sure where to look.

---

## When to Escalate

There will be situations where you simply do not have the permissions, knowledge, or tools necessary to solve a problem.

That is completely normal.

!!! success "Escalation is always okay"
    There is never anything wrong with escalating an issue when it is outside your abilities, when you need additional support, or when you are unsure what to do.

For example:

- A Moderator finds a griefed house but does not have the tools required to investigate the damage.
- An Admin discovers a duplication bug but does not have the tools required to fix the plugin causing it.
- A staff member encounters a technical problem they do not understand.

In these situations, follow the [staff chain of command](../../documentation/staff-roles-responsibilities.md#21-chain-of-command){ data-preview } and ask for help.

!!! important
    **Never avoid escalating an issue because you are worried that you should already know the answer.** Asking for help early is much better than making an incorrect change or taking inappropriate action.

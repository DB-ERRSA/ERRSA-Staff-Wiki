# ERRSA policy consistency pass — Rev11 review v4

This is a **leadership review copy**, not a published change to the active ERRSA procedures. It builds on the Rev11 v3 review package. All disciplinary and authorization decisions below require agreement from the Server Leads before adoption.

## Section 3.2 refinements

1. **Harassment remains §3.2.4** even in text or voice chat. Section 3.2.3 is referenced only for communication-tool behavior, not as a replacement offense ladder.
2. **Spamming and advertising share §3.2.8** as a category separate from inappropriate language. Staff follow the warning/mute steps and durations of §3.2.3, but use only the §3.2.8 disciplinary history to determine the step.
3. **Warnings do not advance numbered mute/ban duration steps.** Formal warnings are documented and can establish continued behavior; the first mute/ban is the first restriction step, the next restriction in the same category is the second, and so on. Repeated warnings should not replace the next action when procedures call for it.
4. **Every short repeat-offense callout links to the central example**, including the new callout in §3.2.8. One short existing Moderator learning tip was aligned to avoid conflicting instructions.

## Admin certification record

The optional supervised-access section now captures Server Lead approval, which temporary permissions were granted, who granted access, when access was removed, and who verified removal. This does not pre-authorize a permission grant.

## Important decisions before approval

- Confirm that numbered disciplinary durations track **mute/ban actions**, not the count of warning letters, and confirm that minor chat, harassment, and spam cases should proceed as described.
- Confirm that §3.2.8 is a separate category borrowing §3.2.3 mute durations.
- Confirm that harassment through chat remains §3.2.4, with no cross-counting to §3.2.3.
- Confirm the v3 voice-chat policy and Server Lead-controlled training access.

## Investigative jailing remains pending

**No active jailing instruction in §3.2.6 or §3.2.7 was changed here.** The separate investigative-jailing proposal remains unapproved and outside published wiki navigation. Its proposed 15-minute review and 30-minute initial limit are not active policy. When adopted, update the affected Standard Procedures and Moderator/Admin training simultaneously.
